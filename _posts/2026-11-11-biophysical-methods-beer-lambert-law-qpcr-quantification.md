Unit 13 (Methods in Biology) consistently contributes $25\text{–}35$ marks in the CSIR NET Life Sciences examination. Two of the most frequently tested quantitative topics are UV-Visible spectrophotometry (Beer-Lambert Law) for protein/nucleic acid purity and real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) relative gene expression analysis.

## 1. Beer-Lambert Law & Molar Extinction Coefficient ($\varepsilon$)

When monochromatic light of intensity $I_0$ passes through an absorbing solution of path length $l$ (in $\text{cm}$) and molar concentration $c$ (in $\text{M}$), the transmitted light intensity $I$ decreases exponentially. **Absorbance ($A$)** (also called optical density, $\text{OD}$) is defined as:

$$A = \log_{10}\left(\frac{I_0}{I}\right) = -\log_{10}(T) = \varepsilon \cdot c \cdot l$$

Where:
* $T = \frac{I}{I_0}$ is the **Transmittance** (often expressed as $\%T = T \times 100$).
* $\varepsilon$ is the **Molar Extinction Coefficient** in $\text{M}^{-1}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}$.

### Useful Shortcut Conversions for CSIR NET:
* If **$\%T = 10\%$**, then $T = 0.1 \implies A = -\log_{10}(0.1) = 1.0$.
* If **$\%T = 1\%$**, then $T = 0.01 \implies A = -\log_{10}(0.01) = 2.0$.
* **Tryptophan ($\text{Trp}$), Tyrosine ($\text{Tyr}$), and Disulfide Bonds ($\text{Cys-Cys}$)** govern protein absorbance at $280 \, \text{nm}$ (Pace formula):
  $$\varepsilon_{280} \, (\text{M}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-1}) \approx (5500 \times n_{\text{Trp}}) + (1490 \times n_{\text{Tyr}}) + (125 \times n_{\text{S-S}})$$

## 2. Nucleic Acid Quantification & Purity Ratios ($A_{260}/A_{280}$)

Purines and pyrimidines absorb strongly at $260 \, \text{nm}$. In a standard $1 \, \text{cm}$ quartz cuvette, an absorbance of $A_{260} = 1.0$ corresponds to:
* **Double-stranded DNA (dsDNA):** $50 \, \mu\text{g/mL}$
* **Single-stranded RNA (ssRNA):** $40 \, \mu\text{g/mL}$
* **Single-stranded DNA (ssDNA / oligos):** $\sim 33 \, \mu\text{g/mL}$

| Purity Ratio | Pure dsDNA | Pure RNA | Diagnostic Interpretation of Deviations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$A_{260} / A_{280}$** | $\sim 1.80$ | $\sim 2.00$ | Ratio $< 1.6$ indicates **protein or phenol** contamination |
| **$A_{260} / A_{230}$** | $2.00 \text{ – } 2.20$ | $2.00 \text{ – } 2.20$ | Ratio $< 1.8$ indicates **guanidinium salts, EDTA, or carbohydrate** carryover |

## 3. Real-Time qPCR Amplification Kinetics & The $2^{-\Delta\Delta C_t}$ Method

During exponential PCR amplification, the copy number $N_n$ after $n$ thermal cycles is governed by the amplification efficiency $E$ (where $E = 1.0$ represents $100\%$ doubling per cycle):

$$N_n = N_0 \times (1 + E)^n$$

When a standard curve is generated by plotting $C_t$ (threshold cycle) on the Y-axis against $\log_{10}(\text{Initial Template Concentration})$ on the X-axis, the slope of the linear regression line determines PCR efficiency:

$$E = 10^{\left(-\frac{1}{\text{Slope}}\right)} - 1$$

An ideal slope of **$-3.322$** corresponds to $10^{\left(1/3.322\right)} - 1 = 2 - 1 = 1.00$ ($100\%$ efficiency, meaning template doubles every cycle and a $10$-fold serial dilution spaces $C_t$ values by exactly $3.32$ cycles).

### Relative Gene Expression (Livak $2^{-\Delta\Delta C_t}$ Method)
To compare the expression of a Target Gene normalized to an internal Reference (Housekeeping) Gene between a Treated sample and an Untreated Control sample:

1. $\Delta C_{t\text{ (Treated)}} = C_{t\text{ (Target, Treated)}} - C_{t\text{ (Reference, Treated)}}$
2. $\Delta C_{t\text{ (Control)}} = C_{t\text{ (Target, Control)}} - C_{t\text{ (Reference, Control)}}$
3. $\Delta\Delta C_t = \Delta C_{t\text{ (Treated)}} - \Delta C_{t\text{ (Control)}}$
4. $\text{Fold Change in Expression} = 2^{-\Delta\Delta C_t}$

## 4. Worked CSIR NET Part C Numerical Problem

**Problem:** A purified monomeric enzyme ($40 \, \text{kDa}$) contains $4$ Tryptophan residues, $6$ Tyrosine residues, and no disulfide bonds. A solution of this enzyme measured in a $1 \, \text{cm}$ path-length cuvette transmits $10\%$ of incident light at $280 \, \text{nm}$. Calculate the protein concentration in $\text{mg/mL}$.

**Solution:**
1. **Calculate $\varepsilon_{280}$:**
   $$\varepsilon_{280} = (5500 \times 4) + (1490 \times 6) = 22,000 + 8,940 = 30,940 \, \text{M}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-1}$$
2. **Convert $10\%$ Transmittance ($T = 0.10$) to Absorbance ($A_{280}$):**
   $$A_{280} = -\log_{10}(0.10) = 1.0$$
3. **Calculate Molar Concentration ($c$):**
   $$c = \frac{A}{\varepsilon \cdot l} = \frac{1.0}{30,940 \times 1} = 3.23 \times 10^{-5} \, \text{M}$$
4. **Convert Molarity to $\text{mg/mL}$ (since $\text{g/L} = \text{mg/mL}$):**
   $$\text{Concentration} = (3.23 \times 10^{-5} \, \text{mol/L}) \times (40,000 \, \text{g/mol}) = 1.29 \, \text{g/L} = 1.29 \, \text{mg/mL}$$
