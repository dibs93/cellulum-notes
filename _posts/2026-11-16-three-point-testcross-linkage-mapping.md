  <h1 class="post-title">Solving Three-Point Testcross &amp; Gene Mapping Problems in Genetics</h1>
  <p class="post-subtitle">A step-by-step framework to identify gene order, calculate double crossover frequencies, and determine interference.</p>

  <p>Genetics questions involving three-point testcrosses are a mandatory fixture in CSIR NET Part C. These problems test your ability to reconstruct chromosome maps using progeny phenotypic distributions from trihybrid crosses.</p>

  <h2>1. The Four Rules of Three-Point Mapping</h2>
  <p>When given a progeny table containing 8 phenotypic classes, follow this systematic workflow:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Step 1: Identify Non-Recombinant (Parental) Classes.</strong> These represent the two largest phenotypic groups because parental combinations occur with the highest frequency.</li>
    <li><strong>Step 2: Identify Double Crossover (DCO) Classes.</strong> These represent the two smallest phenotypic groups, as simultaneous crossover events across both intervals are least likely.</li>
    <li><strong>Step 3: Determine Gene Order.</strong> Compare the DCO phenotypes with the parental phenotypes. The allele that has "switched places" relative to the parents in the DCO class is the gene located in the <em>middle</em>.</li>
    <li><strong>Step 4: Calculate Map Distances.</strong> Recombination frequency ($RF$) between two loci is calculated as:</li>
  </ul>

  $$RF (\%) = \frac{\text{Total Single Crossovers (Interval 1 or 2)} + \text{Double Crossovers}}{\text{Total Progeny}} \times 100$$

  <h2>2. Interference &amp; Coefficient of Coincidence</h2>
  <p>The occurrence of one crossover often suppresses or influences the formation of an adjacent crossover event. This is measured via the Coefficient of Coincidence ($C$):</p>

  $$C = \frac{\text{Observed Double Crossovers}}{\text{Expected Double Crossovers}}$$

  <p>Where <em>Expected DCO</em> is calculated by multiplying the independent single crossover frequencies of interval 1 and interval 2 by the total progeny count. **Interference ($I$)** is then quantified as:</p>

  $$I = 1 - C$$
